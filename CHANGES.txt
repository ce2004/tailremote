1.8.6
New feature: copy and paste between the PCs. Copy files, folders or text with Control C (or Control X) on one PC, then paste with Control V on the other, in any folder or program. Everything travels in the background, folders and all, with text of any size up to 512 megabytes. Copying something new while a transfer is going replaces it. Pasting always copies: nothing on the other PC is ever deleted. This replaces the Send files button. Untick Share the clipboard (Alt B) and each PC keeps its own clipboard: neither ever updates the other, and nothing is sent.
New feature: the File transfer tab (Control Tab). It shows what is being sent or received, how much is done, the speed and the time left, with a Stop the transfer button. NVDA only says when a transfer starts and when it finishes.
Changed: sending files no longer makes the sound lag. While files are going, TailRemote measures the sound's ping ten times a second and slows the transfer the moment it starts holding the sound up, then speeds it back up, so files go as fast as the connection allows with the sound kept clear.
New feature: many more sounds. Real recorded instruments: a Steinway, harp, glockenspiel, marimba, xylophone and pizzicato strings, plus a classic phone and the earlier ones (chime, music box, electric piano, glass, kalimba, bell, text tone, vibraphone), Android's notification and effect sounds, Nepalese bells, and classic phone tunes. Choose the key for all of them (Alt K in Sounds).
Changed: sounds are different each time. Default plays each event's own tune (rising for connecting, a sigh for an error) on a different instrument every time, and Random sound plays anything at all.
Bug fixed: clipboard sharing could bounce the same thing back and forth between the PCs, and copying quickly could make it fall behind. A paste from the other PC is never sent back, and quick copies send only the latest one.
Bug fixed: sharing a large clipboard could freeze TailRemote. Nothing about the clipboard or the sounds runs on the window any more.

1.8.5
Bug fixed: opening Send files could freeze TailRemote until Windows closed it. The Windows file picker looks through every drive and add-on as it opens, and one that was slow froze the whole window. The picker now runs on its own, so TailRemote keeps working however long it takes.
Bug fixed: TailRemote could also freeze while another program was holding the clipboard. The clipboard is now shared in the background.
Bug fixed: a connection that damaged data while logging in (Clumsy's tamper, or a bad link) was taken for a wrong password, so TailRemote gave up reconnecting and the host counted it toward blocking your PC. Every login message now carries a check, so damage is recognised as damage: it just tries again, and the host never counts it.
Bug fixed: damaged data after logging in could leave keys going nowhere for up to 10 seconds before TailRemote noticed. It now notices at once and reconnects.
New feature: sounds for connecting, disconnecting, clipboard sent and received, files sent and received, switching control, and errors. Press Sounds for connecting, clipboard and files (Alt L) to choose a sound for each one, or None. There are 144: a real Steinway piano, soft piano, chime, marimba, music box, harp, electric piano, glass, kalimba, bell, text tone and vibraphone, each playing 12 little patterns in B-flat major. Each one plays as you move to it. The sounds play only on the PC you control from.
Changed: if the window ever stops responding for 3 seconds, TailRemote writes what it was doing to TailRemote-crash.txt next to it, so the cause can be found.

1.8.4
Bug fixed: Send files could close TailRemote, for example if you closed the progress window while files were still going. Closing it now simply cancels the sending, and nothing about sending files can close the app any more.
Changed: files send as fast as your connection allows. Reading, encrypting and sending now all happen at the same time instead of taking turns, on both PCs. On this PC's own connection a 128 MB file went at over 600 megabytes a second, so your network is the only limit. When a transfer finishes, NVDA says how fast it went.
Bug fixed: if the PC receiving files could not save one (a full disk, for example), the transfer could freeze. Now that file is dropped with a message and the rest carry on.
Changed: the audio delay is now worked out from a ping on the sound's own path, not the keyboard connection's, so it stays right even while a file transfer is busy.

1.8.3
Changed: joining on a terrible connection, even dial-up, finds a bitrate that fits within a few seconds instead of trying over and over for 20. It now notices when the sound is arriving slower than real time, which is how a modem or a full connection behaves (it queues the sound rather than losing it), and goes straight for the bitrate the connection can really carry.
Changed: any change of bitrate takes about 3 seconds however far it goes, so a big drop no longer crawls one step at a time, and a small one is still gentle.
Changed: after a slow spell, it holds at the speed the connection was measured to carry for 5 seconds before climbing back, so it no longer climbs straight back into trouble on a modem. On a connection that has really recovered it is back at full quality a few seconds later.

1.8.2
Bug fixed: after a bad stretch of network, the sound could stay at a low bitrate for up to a minute even though the connection was fine again. Now it starts climbing back as soon as the connection is clean, one step every three quarters of a second, and is back to full quality within about 10 seconds even from the very bottom.

1.8.1
Bug fixed: with Sound quality locked, the first half second after connecting played at the highest bitrate. Now you only ever hear the bitrate you locked, from the very first sound, and changing the lock takes effect at once.

1.8.0
Changed: the sound now uses Opus, the codec behind Discord and most voice apps, at up to 510 kilobits per second. At the top it sounds the same as the PC itself, uses far less data than before, and adds only about 2 ms of delay.
New feature: Sound quality (Alt Q, on the controlling PC). Variable follows the connection: on a slow or busy connection it lowers the bitrate until the sound fits, all the way down to 6 kilobits per second, which even works over dial-up, and comes back up when it can. It moves one step at a time, never faster than one step every three quarters of a second, so the sound changes over a few seconds rather than suddenly. Or lock it to any bitrate from 510 down to 6, and it stays there no matter what.
Changed: above 16 kilobits per second the sound is tuned for music; from 16 down it is tuned for speech and can rebuild a lost packet from the next one.
New feature: a Streaming line you reach with Tab. It says what bitrate the sound is coming in at and the audio delay, and updates every second.
Changed: a lost packet is now filled in by Opus itself, which sounds much smoother than before.
Changed: the host stops sending sound to a PC the moment that PC goes quiet, instead of streaming to nobody for up to 10 seconds.
Changed: the Status log box is gone. NVDA already says everything that was in it, and the log file keeps it when logging is on.
Bug fixed: an unexpected error could close TailRemote. Now it is written to TailRemote-crash.txt next to TailRemote, and the app carries on.

1.7.10
Changed: the sound always heads back to live. Nothing ever builds up a delay: if the connection stalls, what piles up behind it is skipped (or fast-forwarded, if you turned that on) the moment it arrives, and a network that simply gets slower no longer adds its delay to the sound for good. Nothing old is ever played: sound that arrives behind is thrown away as if it never existed, and the buffer never goes above 40 ms.
New feature: on a slow or capped connection, the sound steps down in quality until it fits, all the way to 8 kHz mono, and stays at the quality that works instead of bouncing back up and breaking up again. It tries full quality again every 10 seconds.
Changed: Connect keeps trying every 3 seconds until you press Disconnect, instead of giving up. A wrong password still stops it at once.
Bug fixed: fast-forwarding sometimes stopped short and left the sound a little behind.
Changed: both PCs need the newest version.

1.7.9
Bug fixed: the sound was fuzzy, most of all the bass, over Wi-Fi. 1.7.8 threw away good audio whenever packets arrived out of order and a little late, which Wi-Fi does all the time, leaving tiny gaps many times a second. Nothing good is thrown away now.
Bug fixed: the sound quality often dropped and sometimes never came back. Those same thrown-away packets counted as lost. Now only heavy loss (more than a tenth of the sound) lowers the quality, and if a lower quality does not actually help, it goes straight back to full. Scattered loss, like 2 to 5 percent, never lowers it.
Bug fixed: right after connecting, the sound could stop and buffer for a moment. The buffer now sizes itself before the sound starts.
Bug fixed: a packet that really is lost no longer makes a click.
Changed: fast-forwarding sounds much smoother. It plays at 1.5x when a little behind, 2x when further behind and 4x only when far behind, and blends each jump so it sounds like a podcast player sped up rather than chopped pieces. Skipping ahead, the default, is blended the same way, so you should not hear it at all.

1.7.8
Bug fixed: the pitch of the sound wobbled. TailRemote sped the sound up and slowed it down a little to keep its delay steady, and you could hear it. The pitch now never changes. When the sound falls behind, TailRemote skips ahead to catch up, with a tiny fade so it does not click.
New feature: Catch up by fast-forwarding (Alt Y, off at first). Instead of skipping, the sound plays at 2x, or 4x if it is far behind, until it has caught up. It keeps its pitch, so it sounds like a quick jumbled fast-forward rather than chipmunks.
Changed: when the connection gets better, the sound goes back to full quality at once instead of a second later.
Changed: switching between sound qualities now glides from one to the next with no gap, pop or extra delay.
Bug fixed: changing the output that the host captures from could make the sound stutter for a moment.
Bug fixed: if you pressed Control Alt Delete or locked this PC while controlling the other one, Control and Alt could stay held down on the other PC.
Bug fixed: pressing Connect twice quickly could start two connections.
Bug fixed: in Host mode, Alt A meant both Capture sound from and Apply settings to the service. Apply settings to the service is now Alt S.
Bug fixed: if TailRemote closed unexpectedly, it did not always come back hosting or connected after the next start.
Bug fixed: Port editor said its error messages twice with NVDA.
Bug fixed: the service's log was wiped when it got big. The older half is now kept in service.log.old.
Changed: an update is only installed when GitHub gives a checksum to check the download against.

1.7.7
Bug fixed: the sound chopped over Wi-Fi even though every packet arrived. Packets never arrive evenly over Wi-Fi, and the tiny fixed buffer kept running dry and throwing sound away, about a tenth of a second every second. The buffer now sizes itself to how unevenly packets really arrive and then holds steady, so the delay stays the same while the sound stays smooth. Expect about 30 ms on a wired connection and somewhat more over Wi-Fi.

1.7.6
Bug fixed: hosting from some virtual audio devices, such as Virtual Audio Cable, made the sound choppy. TailRemote mistook uneven timing information from the device for gaps and added about a third more sound than there was, which the other PC then had to cut away. Only real gaps count now.
New feature: while connected or hosting, TailRemote keeps Wi-Fi steady. It turns on Windows media streaming mode and pauses background network scans, which made the sound chop whenever the Wi-Fi card was busy. Windows puts both back when TailRemote disconnects or closes.

1.7.5
New feature: Enable logging (Alt G). While it is on, TailRemote writes TailRemote-log.txt next to itself: every connection, every sound problem, and a line every second with how the sound is doing on that PC. What you type is never written down, only how many keys were pressed. For the service, press Apply settings to the service after turning it on.

1.7.4
Bug fixed: the sound kept buffering and catching up, and the delay drifted. TailRemote was cutting the sound at normal small swings and moving its buffer whenever the capture device handed over one odd chunk. Now the delay stays fixed, about 30 ms on a good connection, and only a real pile-up after a network bump is cut.
Bug fixed: when two audio packets arrived swapped, which networks do, one was lost and made a tiny gap, and the sound wrongly dropped to 8 kHz. Swapped packets are now put back in order, and the quality only drops for packets that really never arrive.
Bug fixed: on some PCs Windows ran TailRemote on its slow power-saving cores, which made the sound run dry. TailRemote now asks to run at full speed.

1.7.3
Changed: the TailRemote service no longer checks for updates by itself. It only updates when you update TailRemote on that PC with Check for updates, and then it follows straight away to the same version, with no questions and no administrator prompt.

1.7.2
Bug fixed: capturing sound from a device other than the default lagged and stuttered. Some devices, such as Bluetooth and USB ones, hand over their sound in bigger chunks; the host now measures that and the other PC allows for exactly that much, so the delay stays fixed.
New feature: the TailRemote service updates itself. It checks GitHub every 10 minutes, and straight away after you update TailRemote on that PC, with no questions and no administrator prompt. The service needs this version once; after that it keeps itself up to date.

1.7.1
Bug fixed: if the connection dropped while you were controlling the remote PC, you had to press Control Shift Enter again after it reconnected. Now it goes straight back to controlling the remote PC and says so.
Changed: reconnecting starts at once and tries every second.
Changed: the sound delay is fixed and never grows. Audio that arrives late is skipped instead of waited for, so on a jittery connection you may hear a tiny gap, but never a growing delay.

1.7.0
Changed: the sound reacts to the connection much faster. It steps down within a fifth of a second of trouble, two steps at once when it is bad, and climbs back after a second and a half of clean sound, one step a second. If climbing back brings the trouble straight back, it waits longer before trying again.
New feature: in Host mode, Capture sound from (Alt A) chooses which output the other PCs hear, or Windows default. Changing it while hosting switches straight away. The service uses it too after Apply settings to the service.

1.6.0
Changed: on a slow or struggling connection, the sound now steps down in sample rate instead of detail: 32, 24, 16, 11 and finally 8 kHz, which is phone quality and still perfectly clear for speech. Every step is lossless at its own rate. At 8 kHz it uses a small fraction of the data. It climbs back to full quality after 10 good seconds, and the window title says when the sound is reduced.

1.5.0
New feature: sound uses about a fifth less data again, and is still exactly lossless.
New feature: on a slow or struggling connection, TailRemote drops the finest detail one step at a time (down to 12-bit) instead of letting the sound lag, and goes back to lossless after 10 good seconds. The window title says when it has.
Safer: TailRemote cannot be frozen by someone flooding it with logins, by a PC that stops responding, or by many listeners. After 5 wrong passwords, that address is refused for a minute.
Faster: less work and less memory churn while sound plays.

1.0.0
First release: control another Windows PC over Tailscale with your whole keyboard, and hear everything it plays in 16-bit 44.1 kHz.
Control Shift Enter switches between the remote PC and this one.
The audio buffer sets itself: it measures your connection and keeps the delay as low as it can without breaking up.
A lost audio packet fades out smoothly instead of clicking.
If the connection drops, TailRemote reconnects on its own.
Check for updates downloads the new version and restarts where you left off.
